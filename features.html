The building blocks

Five words carry the whole system. Every feature below is made of them.

B1

Semble

Now

What you bring. One active piece of work, shared in the open.

  • Both the gathering and the reason for it — the thing you make, and the people who come to meet it
  • One at a time. Replacing it archives the last, and nothing is lost
  • Requires a public link: code, a doc, a model, a recording, a craft
B2

Semble Session · a Sesh

Now

One gathering, in one real place, at one real time. The atomic unit of Semble.

  • A Sesh is opened by a person with Init, never programmed by a platform
  • It is not scheduled until the threshold is met — then it is certain, and everyone is told once
  • It runs on a Model (F8): a proven, forkable shape for how the time is spent
  • It mints one fixed pool of !MOTUS, allocated by the people who were actually there
  • Sesh is how people say it. Semble Session is what it is called in the record
B3

Semble Steps

Now

The unit of movement. A Step is one small, named thing you actually do.

  • Before a Sesh — commit your stake, post what you are bringing, read the Sembles you will meet, pack the thing that is stuck
  • At the door — tap the anchor. This Step signs itself, and returns your stake
  • After — allocate your share of the pool, and follow through on what you said in the room
  • Every Step you take lands in your Trax, dated. Steps are the only thing Semble counts
  • A Step is never a streak or a nudge. It is a commitment small enough to keep
  • Next Conveners publish a Step list alongside their Model, so a Sesh arrives with its own preparation attached
B4

Trax

Now

The line your Steps make. Your record and your profile are the same object.

  • Every Sesh attended, every Step taken, every !MOTUS in and out
  • Dated, narrow, scoped claims — signed by the people who were there, checkable by a stranger
  • No score, no rank, no leaderboard, no number. A Trax is read, not compared
  • Exportable in one tap, and it verifies without our servers
  • Nothing in a Trax can be altered after the fact, including by us
B5

!MOTUS

Now

What a room gives you for what you brought.

  • A fixed pool per Sesh, allocated by attendees. It can be redistributed, never inflated
  • Earned in, sent out, held — all three visible in your Trax at all times
  • Buys supervised agent-minutes: agent work run while you are present
  • Its full economic design, including transferability and how it relates to any future token, is an open decision

The product

Fourteen features. Each one exists to make a loop turn.

F1

One Active Semble

Now

Your profile is your work. One active Semble at a time — not a bio, not a portfolio.

  • A title, what you are building, where it lives in the open, and your city
  • The open link is required. Code, a doc, a model, a recording, a craft — anything, as long as it is public
  • A drive holds every past Semble, traced and dated
  • Optionally links to your MotusMove on MotusMoves.US as the long arc it serves
  • Two faces on every profile: the builder (your active work) and the backer (what you have credited, and were present for)
  • Replacing your Semble archives the old one automatically — nothing is lost, but only one is live
F2

Init — open a Semble Session

Now

Init is the first move: anyone opens a Sesh, and the room decides whether it happens. No organiser class, no application.

  • What you convene on, where, when, and the number of people it needs
  • Init costs a returnable stake and commits you to nothing else — it is a hint, not an all-in. The threshold, not the founder, decides if it is real
  • Anywhere real — a library, a museum, a park, a walk, a workshop
  • Under 90 seconds to complete on a phone
  • Requires an active Semble, and a convener bond that funds the refund bonus
  • Pick a Semble Model (F8) or write your own structure
  • Free-text location plus an optional map pin
F3

The Threshold, the Stake and the Refund Bonus

Now

Nothing is scheduled until enough people commit — and committing means something.

  • The stake — a small amount, returned in full the moment you physically arrive. Not a ticket
  • The refund bonus — if the Sesh never fills, everyone who committed gets back more than they put down, funded by the convener's bond. Committing early is the rational move rather than the risky one
  • Live count of who has committed, each shown with their active Semble
  • Confirmation fires exactly once, at threshold, with a calendar handoff
  • Configurable threshold per Sesh, with a community-set floor
  • A Sesh that never fills expires gracefully — the convener is invited to Init it again, never marked as failed
  • Per-person bonus caps, so a Sesh that never fills cannot be farmed
  • Stakes are escrowed per Sesh and never pooled
  • Next Communities can pool staked !MOTUS toward an open Sesh, so the most-wanted Sessions surface by conviction
F4

The Room

Now

The Sesh itself. In person is the point; the app's job is before and after.

  • Ephemeral by default — nothing records
  • Anyone present can mark a Motus Moment; only marked moments are kept
  • Every marked moment shows who marked it, visibly, at the time
  • While a room is live its card holds a slow breath and nothing else moves
  • Next Voice Sembles — live rooms for the community between cities, with marking as the only capture
F5

The Signature, the Trace and the Credit

Now

What a Sesh leaves behind.

  • The room signs its own attendance (see Presence below)
  • A fixed credit pool per Sesh, allocated by attendees to what people brought
  • Only attendees can allocate. The window closes in 48 hours
  • The ledger is append-only
  • Forkable traces — carry a shared model, practice or structure into your own Semble, with origin credited automatically
  • The follow-through gate — you can Init new Sessions only if you showed up to the ones you committed to. Absence throttles opening new Sessions, never attending
  • States: committed → attended / excused / absent. "Excused" is honourable and carries no penalty
F6

Human Introductions

Now

The matching system is people, and possibly always will be.

  • You can refer someone to a Sesh only if you attended that kind of room yourself
  • Every referral carries your name — it is a vouch, not a share link
  • Referrals that lead to real attendance earn $DASH, wallet to wallet
  • Settlement happens on attendance, never on a click
  • The person referred always sees who vouched for them, and that person's Semble
  • No algorithm decides who you meet. Suggestions come from people who were there, or from your own agent reading your own record
F7

Privacy Controls

Now

Two modes, legible enough to teach.

  • Open — your Semble, traces and record are public to people
  • Held — visible only to people you have shared a room with
  • Per item: readable by people only, or people and agents. Agent readability defaults off
  • One tap to change, with a plain-language consequence shown at the moment of change
  • "Who can see this" is always one tap away and always accurate
  • Exactly one intelligence reads your full record: your own agent
F8

Semble Models

Now

The shape of a Sesh, as a forkable building block.

  • Five seed Models ship at v0: Build Session · Pitch Circle · Walk-and-Talk · Workshop · Show-Your-Practice
  • Each defines roles, flow, timing, and what gets marked
  • Fork and edit any Model; the origin is credited automatically
  • Every confirmed Sesh displays the Model it runs
  • Models read as documents, not configuration screens
  • Next Publishing opens to everyone
  • Season Proven Models graduate into minted MotusModels on Motus.Market — same credit law, one lineage
F9

The Living Block

Next

Your work is not a slide. It is live in the room, in other people's hands.

  • Every active Semble carries a one-tap live link that opens on a second device
  • The people present can open it, try it, and touch it while you talk
  • Their interactions return to you afterward as a trace overlay, attributed to the hands that made them
  • Only marked touches are captured — the same rule as Motus Moments
  • Feedback stops being talk and becomes an artifact
F10

The Stream

Live today

The digital room, already shipped on semble.cc.

  • A builder works in the open; viewers join and contribute from their device
  • Streams are Sessions: moments get marked, credit gets allocated, traces are forkable
  • Contribution is opt-in with a device budget, and metered visibly for both sides
  • Stated plainly: contributed device compute is a presence and contribution signal. It does not run the agents — volunteer-device inference is far too slow for interactive work. Agent inference is bought from real providers
F11

Trax — the verified build record

Now

Evidence, not a score — and the distinction is the whole design.

  • Every attested Sesh, kept commitment, allocated credit and credited fork becomes a narrow, dated, scoped claim
  • It reads like a receipt: "Ana credited this build, on this date, in a room she was verifiably in."
  • There is no score. No rank, no leaderboard, no profile number, and no API that computes one
  • One-tap export of the whole set
  • Independently verifiable without Semble's servers
  • The schema is published and open source
  • Nothing in the record can be altered after the fact — including by us
F14

Day one

Now

The state a person actually meets first — designed and seeded, not a fallback.

  • No Semble, no Steps, no Trax, no !MOTUS, nothing scheduled — and every screen still legible
  • One entry requirement: keep a Semble, a title and a public link. Nothing else is gated
  • Onboarding is Steps, not a tour — the same primitive the rest of the app runs on
  • Taking a seat without a Semble is refused with a reason and a route, never a dead end
  • A kept-rate with no commitments behind it reads —, never a flattering 100%
  • The Trax line still draws, dimmed, so the shape is legible before there is content in it
  • The preview switches between day one and a six-week-old account in one tap
F12

!MOTUS history, in and out

Now

Every movement of credit you have made or received, in one place.

  • Held — your balance right now, the number that decides what compute you can run
  • Earned in — every allocation a room made to you, with the Sesh, the date, and what it was for
  • Sent out — every allocation you made to someone else, and every credit spent on agent-minutes
  • Filterable to received or sent, and every row names the person or the Sesh — never an anonymous transaction id
  • Model origin credit lands here automatically when someone forks something of yours
  • The whole ledger is append-only, and exports with your Trax

The presence layer

How a Sesh produces a record of who was there that a stranger can verify.

P1

The Anchor

Now
  • Every Sesh has one physical anchor — an NFC card or token held by the convener
  • Attendees tap it. The anchor signs with a key that cannot be extracted from the hardware
  • A rotating nonce changes every 60–120 seconds, so a signature can neither be minted before the Sesh nor replayed after it
  • Anchor custody rotates between conveners
P2

Peer Co-Signing

Now
  • After tapping, your phone collects co-signatures from at least three other attendees
  • Co-signers must themselves hold valid anchor signatures from the same window
  • A group that never touched the anchor forms a disconnected component and is discarded
  • People sign for people. A phone can be carried by a friend; a link can be forwarded
P3

SafeStep — signing and keys

Next
  • Your key is generated on your device and never leaves it
  • Unlocking requires you. Semble never receives biometric data — the biometric unlocks a local key and nothing more
  • Signs three things: your attendance, your agent's actions in your name, and multi-signature operations
  • Semble cannot sign for you, recover your key, or act as you
  • The high-stakes agent tier stays deferred until this ships
P4

What the record contains

Now
  • Kept: that the Sesh happened, who attested, for whom, and in which window
  • Held by you, disclosed selectively, on an open attestation standard
  • The public graph shows only that a Sesh existed, how many verified attendees, and the credit pool

Never captured:

  • Location or GPS coordinates
  • Raw radio logs or nearby-device scans
  • Biometrics
  • Photos or recordings
  • How long you stayed, or who you stood near
  • A permanent public list of everywhere you have been
P5

Keeping the record accurate

Now
  • Fixed pool per Sesh — allocation can redistribute, never inflate
  • Cross-cluster dilution — a pool scales with the diversity of attendees' prior co-attendance, so a closed loop meeting weekly earns a decaying pool
  • Staked claims — the convener stakes $TRUST on the Sesh; anyone can stake against it
  • Anomalies go to people, never to an automatic penalty

Agents and compute

Davara is the working reference — a live operator with a published baseline, a profile, and a record of work done under her own name.

A1

Presence-gated agency

Next
  • Every tool call is classified across three axes: private data, untrusted content, external communication
  • Two of the three — the agent runs freely
  • All three — hard-gated on live, attested human presence
  • Every consequential action carries a signature resolving to a person, plus a per-invocation token carrying that person's authority
A2

Designed against rubber-stamping

Next

A checkbox is not oversight, and the research says explanations do not fix automation bias. So the friction is structural.

  • The agent states the reversibility of an action before taking it
  • A diff before act on anything consequential
  • Approval rate limiting, so mechanically clearing a queue is impossible
  • Sampled withholding — on a published fraction of decisions the agent gives evidence but no recommendation
A3

Compute credits

Next
  • Denominated in supervised agent-minutes — a unit you can reason about
  • Earned by taking Steps and attending Sessions; spent while present
  • A paid layer sits above the earned floor
  • Physical presence is among the strongest Sybil resistance available, because bodies do not scale the way accounts do

Roles and conduct

Six roles, named plainly. Nobody is only one of them, and moving between them is normal.

R

The roles

  • The Builder — keeps an active Semble and brings it into rooms
  • The Backer — credits work they were present for, with their name attached
  • The Convener — Inits and holds Semble Sessions, and posts the bond
  • The Contributor — gives compute, a venue, a skill, an hour
  • The Investor — a professional deploying capital and judgment, under a distinct protocol
  • The Steward — holds the code, resolves disputes, answers for conduct
C

The code

  • Composure — disagree hard, in good faith, without contempt
  • Your name on it — vouches, credits and attestations all carry a name. Anonymity is for privacy, not for influence
  • No pitching people who did not come to be pitched
  • Consent before contact — every introduction needs a yes from both sides
  • Credit what you build on
  • Follow through, or say you cannot. Silence is what costs
  • Enforcement is by people, in the open, with an appeal. Every action names the steward and the clause

Capital

Investing well is a craft practiced under real accountability. The protocol is built to be worth a professional's time.

K1

What Semble offers

Next
  • The verified build record — what someone said they would do, whether they showed, what they brought, and what the room credited
  • Founder-initiated introductions, always, and free — Semble takes nothing on them
  • Semble Sessions and Motus Events, attended under the same code as everyone
  • Weighted on follow-through, deliberately not on capital already raised
  • Export and integration into the tools a firm already runs on
  • A flat annual subscription. Never per deal, never per introduction
K2

The guarantees, both ways

  • Introductions are free. Semble is never paid a success fee, carry or equity tied to any introduction or round, so the signal it gives you is independent of the outcome
  • The investment happens between you and the founder. Semble never hosts, escrows, signs or processes it
  • Founders reach out first, always. Nobody is presented to you who did not ask to be, and nobody pays to be in front of you
  • Consent is per field, visible, and revocable. Nothing about a builder is published or sold without their explicit yes
  • Investors are addressed as principals — no ranking, no tiers, no badges, no leaderboards
  • Signal is weighted on follow-through, deliberately not on capital already raised, so prior funding never quietly becomes the ranking

Revenue

Motus.Market carries the weight of profit, so the door here can stay open. Every fee fits in one sentence.

M

The flows

  • Motus.Market fees — the six published fees, already live
  • The Semble subscription — free forever to keep a Semble, take your Steps, Init, commit, attend, credit and be introduced. Paid depth: the full traced drive, publishing Models, and tools for frequent hosts
  • The Motus Mover subscription — one identity and one record across the ecosystem
  • Referral earnings — people paid in $DASH for bringing people into real rooms
  • The Trax subscription — professional access, flat annual, export included
  • Compute credits — the paid layer above the earned floor
  • Season Pitch Day — builders whose record opened the door, presenting to investors who came to listen. Free to both sides

The design system

D

The laws

  • Mobile-first at 380px, tap parity, targets at least 44px
  • Contrast computed in both modes at roughly 7:1. Secondary text is near-bone or near-ink, never grey
  • Sora for display, Inter for body, IBM Plex Mono for labels at 13px and up
  • Body text 18px and up; headlines large; strokes 2.5px and up
  • Motion is anticipation, action, settle — never linear. Only transform, opacity, shadow, filter and stroke animate
  • Captions state plainly what a thing does or what just happened. Never aphorisms
  • Never a sharp corner. Never a highlight box on an interactive mark
  • 60fps on a mid-range phone, or the least essential layer is cut
  • The mark — two curves turning into each other, lit where they meet — appears at three moments only
